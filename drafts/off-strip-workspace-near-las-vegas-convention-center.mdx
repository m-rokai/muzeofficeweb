## Why off-Strip beats the alternatives

### Hotel lobbies and business centers

During convention week, every Strip hotel lobby becomes an unofficial coworking space — except without desks, outlets, privacy, or quiet. The business centers are worse: outdated computers, slow printers, per-page charges, and a line of 10 people waiting for the one working workstation.

At Muze Office, you walk in, sit at a real desk, connect to dedicated fast WiFi, and start working. No competing with 50,000 other convention attendees for bandwidth.

### Convention center work areas

Most conventions set up temporary work lounges — rows of tables near the meeting rooms. During small shows, these work. During CES, SEMA, NAB, or any major event, they're packed before 9am and the WiFi is shared with the entire convention floor.

Muze Office is off the convention corridor entirely. Our space doesn't fill up during show week because we're not on the attendee walking path.

### Airport lounges

Priority Pass and Centurion lounges at Harry Reid International are designed for travelers killing time, not for people running a business day. The seating is lounge furniture, the WiFi is shared, and private calls are impossible when the gate announcements happen every 90 seconds. A Muze Office day pass costs $25 — often less than an airport lounge day pass — and gives you a real workspace.

## How to get here from the convention center

The Las Vegas Convention Center is at the north end of the Strip near Paradise Rd and Convention Center Dr.

- **Drive:** Take I-15 S to I-215 W, exit at Bermuda Rd. About 15 minutes in normal traffic.
- **Rideshare:** $15-25 from the LVCC depending on surge pricing during conventions.
- **From Strip hotels:** 10-15 minutes via I-15 S or Las Vegas Blvd.
- **Parking:** Free at Muze Office. Always. No validation, no meters, no garage fees.

## When to use off-Strip workspace

The highest-value use cases for off-Strip workspace during a Las Vegas convention:

1. **Private client or investor meetings** that need a real conference room, not a hotel hallway
2. **Team daily standups** where 4-6 people debrief the day's leads and plan tomorrow
3. **Follow-up work** — emails, proposals, order processing — that requires focus and a real desk
4. **Phone calls and video meetings** with your home office or clients who aren't at the show
5. **Prep work** for the next day's presentations, demos, or pitches
6. **Contract and deal review** that needs privacy and a printer
