## What actually matters in a Houston virtual office

Forget the marketing copy. When you need a commercial mailing address, the provider has to:

1. **Be a real commercial street address** with a suite number — not a UPS Store counter, not a residential conversion in the Heights or Bellaire.
2. **Disclose staffed hours and package rules** so you know who can receive which carriers and signatures.
3. **Include USPS Form 1583 handling** as standard — without it, no commercial mail receiving agency can legally accept your mail.
4. **Be willing to receive UPS, FedEx, and Amazon** if you sell anything physical.
5. **Forward mail on a predictable schedule** if you live outside Houston.
6. **Stay reachable** through a published support channel with clear response times.

## Red flags when shopping Houston virtual offices

- **No physical building tour offered.** If you can't visit the address, the building probably isn't what they claim.
- **Unclear address classification.** Ask whether it is a CMRA, what suite format is used, and which services are actually provided on site.
- **No mention of Form 1583.** Either they're cutting corners or you'll get blindsided after signup.
- **Handling fees omitted from the headline price.** Ask for scanning, forwarding, postage, storage, pickup, and cancellation fees in writing.
- **A contract term you did not expect.** Compare the total commitment, renewal terms, and cancellation notice rather than assuming every provider is month-to-month.
- **Unverified claims about banks, filings, or Google eligibility.** The recipient or platform decides whether an address is accepted.
- **Suburban strip-mall address dressed up as "Galleria area."** Verify the zip code and physical location on Google Maps.

## How to evaluate any Houston provider

Run this checklist before you sign up anywhere:

1. Search the address on Google Maps. Does it resolve to a real commercial Houston building?
2. Verify the address and neighborhood on an official map rather than relying on a marketing label.
3. Look up the building's tenants. Is the operator a legitimate suite-holder?
4. Call the reception number during business hours. Does a human answer?
5. Ask what's included in the base plan vs. added per-piece fees.
6. Ask whether they're a registered CMRA with USPS.
7. Ask to see the full Form 1583 process before signing up.
8. Read the cancellation terms.
