## Compare the full cost, not just the rent

The monthly rent is only one part of an office decision. A [small-business leasing checklist from New Jersey's business portal](https://business.nj.gov/pages/leasing-tips) says to review lease length, tenant improvements (building changes for your business), building costs, and renewal rules. A [SCORE lease guide](https://media.score.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/05064048/Business-SCORE-Card-eBook_0-2-June.pdf) also says to ask about power, repairs, and changes to the space before signing.

### Base rent and building costs

With a standard lease, confirm the space used to calculate rent, yearly increases, renewal options, and any free-rent period. Also ask whether the quote includes building costs or passes them to you.

Some leases charge tenants for operating expenses. These are shared building costs such as insurance, property taxes, or upkeep. CAM means common-area maintenance, such as care of lobbies, halls, and other shared areas. Charges differ by building, so ask how estimates are set and fixed.

Muze private-office pricing uses a custom quote based on room size, site, and available offices. The monthly rate depends on the space you choose.

### Deposit and personal guaranty

A landlord may ask for a security deposit, financial records, a letter of credit, or a personal guaranty. A personal guaranty means an owner promises to cover the business debt if the business cannot pay. Ask how much is due, when it can be returned, and what happens after a missed payment.

Muze's planned Houston private-office terms describe no personal guaranty. Confirm that point in the final contract before signing.

### Buildout and furniture

Standard office space may need design work, permits, cabling, construction, signs, and desks. A tenant-improvement allowance is money a landlord offers for changes made for your business. Ask who approves the work, who pays costs above the allowance, and who owns the changes later.

The planned private-office service is built around a ready, lockable room. Muze's listed office options include a desk, chair, and storage for smaller rooms, with team and larger custom offices also planned. Ask about special equipment, signs, or layout changes instead of assuming they are part of the plan.

### Wi-Fi, utilities, cleaning, and repairs

Depending on the lease, a standard tenant may arrange or pay for Wi-Fi, power, cleaning, repairs, and equipment maintenance. Ask who handles outages, heating and cooling problems, trash service, and repairs inside the space.

For the planned Houston private-office bundle, Wi-Fi, utilities, cleaning, and meeting rooms are listed in the plan. A member would not normally set up extra internet or utility accounts for this office service. Confirm the service level and any exceptions in the final contract. Taxes, insurance, software, gear, and outside help are not part of that list.

## Address and mail handling are not the same as every virtual-office tier

The planned private-office membership lists a business address and mail handling. That may help with business mail, contracts, and forms that accept a business mailing address. The exact mail types, pickup process, mail forwarding, and package rules still need confirmation.

A private office is not the same as every virtual-office plan. A virtual office is the address-first service, and its tiers may differ on letter mail, packages, mail forwarding, pickup, and workspace credits. A private office adds a real workspace, plus the address and mail functions listed for that plan. Do not assume it includes every virtual-office feature.

Keep these address questions separate:

- **Business mailing address:** an address used to receive business mail where the recipient permits it.
- **Texas registered office and registered agent:** the Texas address and person or company chosen to receive legal papers for an entity. A registered agent is a separate role.
- **IRS address:** the mailing or physical address requested on a particular IRS form. Read the form instructions for the field you are filling out.
- **Google Business Profile:** eligibility depends on how the business operates at the location and on Google's rules. A mailing address alone is not enough.

Confirm the rule with the agency, bank, marketplace, or platform before using any address.

## When a standard lease may be better

A private office is not always the better choice. A standard lease may fit better when you need:

- **Control:** You want to choose the layout, wiring, signs, furniture, security, and daily procedures within the building's rules.
- **Custom construction:** Your work needs special rooms, equipment, client flow, or a branded space that a furnished office cannot provide.
- **A larger stable space:** Your team is established and needs more room than a small or larger custom office can support.
- **Long-term cost:** You have the cash and a clear plan to compare a longer term on its own cost.
- **Special facilities:** You need dedicated storage, loading, production, or another facility that shared workspace does not offer.

More control also means more responsibility. Before signing, model the first-year cost and the cost of moving if your team grows, shrinks, or changes direction.
