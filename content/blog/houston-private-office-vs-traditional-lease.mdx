## The total-cost categories to compare

Comparing only the advertised monthly rent can hide the real decision. A [small-business leasing checklist from New Jersey's official business portal](https://business.nj.gov/pages/leasing-tips) recommends paying attention to items such as lease length, tenant improvements, operating expenses, and renewal terms. A [SCORE commercial-lease guide](https://media.score.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/05064048/Business-SCORE-Card-eBook_0-2-June.pdf) likewise treats operating costs, utilities, maintenance, and tenant improvements as questions to resolve before signing.

### Base rent

With a traditional lease, base rent is the visible starting point. Confirm the rentable square footage, annual increases, renewal options, free-rent period, and whether the quoted rate is gross, modified gross, or something else.

### CAM and other operating charges

Many commercial leases pass through some combination of common-area maintenance, property taxes, insurance, or other operating expenses. The exact structure varies, so “CAM” should never be assumed to mean the same thing in every building. Ask what is included in the quoted rent, how estimates are reconciled, and whether the tenant can audit or contest charges.

A planned private-office membership is intended to replace that multi-line occupancy budgeting with a stated monthly bundle. Insurance, taxes, software, equipment, and professional services remain your responsibility; the published inclusions identify the workspace infrastructure Muze plans to provide.

### Deposit and personal guaranty

A traditional landlord may request a security deposit, financial statements, a letter of credit, or a personal guaranty. The amount and release conditions are negotiated. Read the guaranty and default provisions separately from the rent section; they determine what happens if the business changes course.

The planned Houston private-office copy describes month-to-month terms with no personal guaranty. Final Houston documents are not available yet, so treat that as a planned feature to confirm before signing, not as a current promise.

### Buildout and furniture

Traditional space may require design work, permits, cabling, construction, signage, furniture, and a delayed move-in. Even if a landlord offers a tenant-improvement allowance, you need to understand the allowance, approval process, overages, and who owns the improvements at the end of the lease.

The planned Houston private-office membership is designed around a furnished, lockable office. Current Muze data lists a desk, chair, and storage for smaller office types, with team and custom-suite options for larger groups. Ask about specialized equipment, signage, or layout requests rather than assuming they are included.

### Wi-Fi, utilities, cleaning, and maintenance

These are the costs that make an office usable after the keys change hands. A traditional tenant may need to arrange internet service, electricity, janitorial service, repairs, and equipment maintenance, or may pay some of them through operating expenses. Confirm who handles outages, after-hours access, HVAC issues, waste removal, and repairs inside the premises.

## Address and mail handling: a private-office function, not every virtual-office feature

The planned private-office membership includes a professional business address and mail handling in Muze's published service data. That can be useful for business correspondence, contracts, and filings that accept a commercial mail-receiving address.

It is not the same thing as every virtual-office tier. A virtual office is the address-first product, and its individual tiers may differ on letter mail, package receiving, forwarding, pickup, or workspace credits. A private office gives you an actual workspace plus the listed address and mail functions; it should not be assumed to include every virtual-office package or forwarding feature.

The same distinction applies to legal and platform requirements. A business mailing address is not automatically a Texas registered office or registered agent. An IRS mailing address and a physical business location can serve different purposes. Google Business Profile eligibility depends on how the business operates at the location and Google's rules. Confirm the specific requirement with the agency, bank, marketplace, or platform before using any address.

## Where a traditional lease can win

A private office is not automatically better. A traditional lease may be the stronger fit when you need:

- **Control over the premises:** You want to set the layout, cabling, signage, furniture, security, and operating procedures within the building's rules.
- **Custom buildout:** Your work requires specialized rooms, equipment, client flow, or a branded environment that a furnished office cannot provide.
- **A larger stable footprint:** Your team is established, your headcount is predictable, and you need more space than a small-office or custom-suite model can reasonably support.
- **Long-term economics:** You have the capital, operating plan, and confidence to evaluate a longer commitment on its own financial terms.
- **Exclusive infrastructure:** You need dedicated storage, loading, production, or other facilities that a shared workspace is not designed to provide.

The tradeoff is that control usually brings more responsibility. Before signing, model the first-year cash requirement and the cost of being wrong if the team grows, shrinks, relocates, or changes its operating model.
