If you're a founder, e-commerce seller, or out-of-state owner who needs a Texas presence, a [virtual office in Houston](/houston-virtual-office) is the cheapest legitimate way to get there. You get a real Galleria-area street address that satisfies Texas LLC filings, Google Business Profile, and most banks — without leasing a Houston office you'll never sit at.

A virtual office is a commercial street address at a real staffed building where staff receive and scan your mail. The legal distinction that matters: it's a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) address, not a PO Box and not a residential one. The street-address part is what lets it pass on LLC filings, Google verification, and bank KYC checks where a PO Box gets rejected.

Total setup time is about a week, mostly waiting on notarization. The realistic timeline, so you can plan filings around it:

| Address goes live | Provider activates | Same day after 1583 |

| Google Business Profile verification | Postcard or video review | 5–14 days |

The address itself is usable the moment Form 1583 is on file — the longer waits are notarization and Google.

## Step 2 — Sign up and complete USPS Form 1583

Federal law (39 CFR 265.6, the USPS rule governing CMRAs) requires a notarized USPS Form 1583 before any commercial mail receiving agency can accept mail on your behalf. Any legitimate provider sends you the form when you sign up. You'll need:

- A photo ID (driver's license or passport)
- A second ID (utility bill, lease, or second photo ID)
- A notary — online services like NotaryCam or Notarize work; so does any UPS Store in Houston

A few details people get wrong: list **both** an individual name and the business entity on the form if mail will arrive in either, name every person authorized to receive mail (a co-founder or accountant won't be able to pick up your mail otherwise), and make sure the notary's commission hasn't expired — an expired stamp is the most common reason a 1583 bounces back. Upload the notarized form to your provider. From that point your address is live, and you can immediately use the suite-level street address on filings even while Google verification is still pending.

## Step 3 — Update your Texas LLC and state registrations

Use your provider's commercial street address — suite number included — as your business address on:

- Texas Secretary of State LLC filings (Certificate of Formation, Public Information Report)
- IRS EIN application (Form SS-4)
- Texas franchise tax registration
- Sales tax permit (if applicable)
- TREC, MLS, or licensing board records (real estate / insurance pros)

Your registered agent is a separate role — a virtual office is your business address, not your registered agent. If you don't have a Texas registered agent yet, services like Northwest, ZenBusiness, or a Texas attorney handle that piece.

One Texas-specific thing to plan for: the franchise tax. For the 2026 report the no-tax-due threshold is $2.65 million in annualized total revenue, so most small LLCs owe **$0**. But you still have to file — Texas requires a Public Information Report (PIR) every year, due May 15, and your virtual office address is the entity address that goes on it. Missing the PIR, not the tax, is what pushes small LLCs into "forfeited" status. (Out-of-state owners sometimes pick Nevada partly to skip this filing entirely, since Nevada has no state income, corporate income, or franchise tax on most small businesses. ## Step 4 — Verify your Google Business Profile

A virtual office in Houston is one of the few address types Google Business Profile will accept that isn't a leased commercial space. Submit the address, request verification (postcard or video), and your provider receives the verification mail at the suite. A good one notifies you without opening it and lets you pick it up or have it forwarded.

GBP verification typically takes 5–14 days end to end.

## Step 5 — Update your bank and payment processors

Most major banks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Frost Bank) accept commercial mail receiving agency addresses on Texas business checking accounts. Online-first banks (Mercury, Brex) prefer them. A few smaller community lenders won't. Check before switching. Stripe, Square, and PayPal accept the address with documentation showing you've completed Form 1583.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Filing the LLC with a residential address first.** It becomes public record permanently. Use the virtual office address from the start.
- **Skipping Form 1583.** Federal law — non-negotiable. Mail won't be accepted until it's on file.
- **Listing the virtual office as your registered agent.** It isn't. Get a separate registered agent.
- **Using a UPS Store PMB instead.** Reads as a retail mailbox on filings; banks and payment processors increasingly flag these during verification.
- **Forgetting to update existing accounts.** USPS forwarding doesn't cover business mail forever.

## Houston virtual office FAQ

**Is a virtual office address legal for a Texas LLC?**
Yes. Texas lets you use a commercial street address as your business address on the Certificate of Formation and Public Information Report. What you can't do is use it as your registered agent's address unless that agent actually maintains a presence there — those are two separate roles.

**Does a virtual office work for Google Business Profile?**
A virtual office is one of the few non-leased address types Google will sometimes verify, but Google increasingly requires video verification and may not display the address publicly for a service-area business. It's reliable for the LLC and banking pieces; treat the GBP listing as a "maybe" rather than a guarantee.
