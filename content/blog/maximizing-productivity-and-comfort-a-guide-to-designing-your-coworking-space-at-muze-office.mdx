But "designing your space" means something different in a coworking environment than it does in a home office or a private suite. You don't control the HVAC, the floor plan, or the person two desks over. What you *can* control is how you choose, set up, and use the space around you.

## Start by matching the seat to the task

The single biggest design decision in a coworking space isn't your chair or your monitor riser. It's choosing the *right type of space* for the work in front of you. Most productivity complaints in shared offices trace back to a mismatch: someone trying to take three back-to-back client calls from an open hot desk, or paying for a private office to do work that's mostly heads-down email.

Designing your workday around the *right* space is cheaper and more effective than over-buying square footage.

## Build a portable "desk kit"

Because you don't own the furniture in a coworking space, the smartest move is to make your *setup* portable rather than trying to permanently customize a shared surface. A few items that travel in a single bag will do more for comfort than anything bolted to a wall:

- A laptop stand to raise your screen to eye level, plus an external keyboard and mouse so your wrists stay neutral. This is the cheapest fix for the neck and shoulder strain that comes from hunching over a laptop all day.
- Noise-canceling headphones. In an open room, these are your soundproofing. Muze provides enclosed phone booths for calls, but headphones handle the ambient hum of a busy floor for everyday focus.
- A small power bank or your own short charging cable, so you're never tethered to the one outlet near the wall.

Set this kit up the same way every time and your body learns the posture; you stop "deciding" how to sit and just start working.

## Use the building, not just your desk

A coworking space is designed as a whole environment, and the productivity gains come from using all of it rather than treating your desk as an island.

- **Use the cafe as a deliberate context switch.** Muze has an on-site cafe. Moving to a different setting for a coffee or a low-stakes task is a legitimate productivity tool, not a distraction; it resets attention between deep-work blocks.
- **Reserve a room *before* you need it.** Meeting rooms book up around busy periods, especially when a convention is in town. The Las Vegas Convention Center is roughly ten minutes away, so demand spikes during major shows. If you know a client call is coming, book the room earlier in the week.
- **Lean on 24/7 access for your real peak hours.** Members get biometric 24/7 entry, so you can design your schedule around when *you* focus best, not around standard office hours. Free on-site parking and a two-minute drive to I-215 make early-morning or late-night sessions practical.

## Manage distractions with rhythm, not just gear

Headphones and booths handle noise, but the harder distraction in any shared space is social: interruptions and the pull to chat. Design a simple, visible signal for yourself, such as headphones on means heads-down, and stick to it. Batch your collaborative and social time into specific windows (lunch, late afternoon) so the rest of your day stays protected. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can I personalize a desk at a coworking space?**
At a shared or hot desk, keep personalization to what fits in your bag, since the desk may be used by someone else tomorrow. If you want to leave up photos, a second monitor, or supplies permanently, that's what a [private office or dedicated desk](/las-vegas-private-office) is for; you get a consistent, lockable base you can set up once.

**How do I take a private call without a separate office?**
Use one of the phone booths, which are included for members. They're built for exactly this, so you don't have to step outside or whisper through a meeting.
