The SEMA Show is the largest automotive specialty-equipment trade event in the world, and for one week every November it takes over the Las Vegas Convention Center. If you're exhibiting, buying, or covering the show, the hardest part of the week often isn't the floor — it's finding a quiet place to actually close the deals the floor generates.

## How big is SEMA, and when is it?

SEMA 2026 runs **November 3-6, 2026** at the Las Vegas Convention Center, 3150 Paradise Road. It is a trade-only event for the automotive aftermarket — manufacturers, distributors, retailers, installers, and media — and only opens to the general public on the final day, Friday, November 6 ([semashow.com](https://www.semashow.com/)).

To put the crush in perspective: the 2025 SEMA Show drew more than 153,000 attendees from 140 countries, with roughly 2,300 exhibiting brands and over 2,500 members of the global media, per the [Specialty Equipment Market Association](https://www.sema.org/news-media/press-release/sema-show-2025-global-stage-ideas-products-and-passion). Buyer attendance alone runs north of 60,000. That volume swallows the city's workspace infrastructure for the week — hotel lobbies become unofficial coworking floors, business centers grow lines, and the cafes near the LVCC fill by mid-morning.

## Why SEMA attendees need off-site workspace

Unlike a keynote-driven tech show, SEMA is an order-writing event built around the people who keep the aftermarket moving: product managers, purchasing buyers, regional sales reps, distributor principals, and installers comparing lines for the next model year. The conversations start at the booth, but they get closed in a conference room. Specifically:

- **Supplier and dealer negotiations** need a door that closes — not a booth where the competitor across the aisle can hear your volume pricing or your margins
- **Order and contract reviews** need a quiet desk and uninterrupted time to read terms, MAP policies, and freight schedules before you sign
- **Distributor and rep calls** happen between floor shifts, and a hotel room has bad acoustics while the booth has 80 dB of background noise
- **Exhibitor team standups** for the crew working the booth need a room where four to six people can debrief the day's leads and plan tomorrow without shouting
- **Media and content work** — editing photo and video from the floor, filing show coverage, uploading product launches — needs real upload bandwidth, not a throttled hotel network

Because SEMA closes to the public for three of its four days, much of the genuine business happens in private, off-floor settings anyway. A workspace a few minutes from the LVCC is where that business gets done.
