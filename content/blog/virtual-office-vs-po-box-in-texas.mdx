## What each one actually is

A **PO Box** is a numbered slot inside a Houston-area USPS post office. The address looks like `PO Box 12345, Houston TX 77019`. You receive USPS mail only — no UPS, no FedEx, no Amazon packages — and you have to drive in to collect it.

A **virtual office** is a real commercial street address inside an actual staffed building, with a suite number and the option of mail handling, package receiving, and meeting space. The address reads like `1800 Augusta Dr, Houston TX 77057` — a Galleria-area building, indistinguishable from a leased office on filings and contracts. ## The Texas rule that settles most of this

Texas does not let you use a PO Box where it matters most: the registered agent. Section 5.201 of the Texas Business Organizations Code requires every entity's registered office to be a **physical street address in Texas** where the agent can be served with legal process during business hours. The Secretary of State is explicit that a registered office may not be a mailbox service or a telephone answering service. A PO Box fails on both counts — it isn't a physical office, and nobody is there to accept service.

That single rule is why so many Houston founders who started with a PO Box end up refiling. You can sometimes list a PO Box as a separate *mailing* address, but the *registered office* — the one that keeps your LLC in good standing — has to be a staffed street address. A virtual office in a real building satisfies it; a PO Box does not.

## What setup actually involves (and why it's not instant)

Whichever route you pick, mail at a virtual office is federally regulated. Before any virtual office, virtual mailbox, or mail-forwarding provider can legally receive mail for you, USPS requires a completed **Form 1583** ("Application for Delivery of Mail Through Agent"), signed in front of a notary and backed by two forms of ID — one a government-issued photo ID. Most providers now handle the notarization over live video, so plan for roughly a week, mostly spent waiting on that step. A PO Box skips Form 1583 but gives you none of the LLC, banking, or package benefits in return. ## When a PO Box is fine in Texas

A PO Box is the right call if you:

- Run a hobby business or side project that won't be filed as an LLC
- Only need a private address for personal mail
- Don't need to receive packages from any commercial carrier
- Aren't trying to get verified on Google Business Profile or open a business bank account

If that's you, save the $25–$30 a month — the cheaper option genuinely does the job.

## When you need a virtual office in Houston instead

Start with a virtual office whenever you're:

- Filing a Texas LLC, corporation, or DBA (and need a compliant registered office)
- Setting up a Google Business Profile to show up in Houston local search
- Opening a Texas business checking account
- Selling products that ship via UPS, FedEx, or Amazon
- Listing an address on contracts, invoices, or a public-facing website
- Working with payment processors (Stripe, Square, PayPal) that verify business addresses
- Holding a Texas professional license (TREC, insurance, etc.)

PO-Box-first founders we've seen typically end up filing an LLC amendment, re-verifying Google Business Profile, and re-opening their bank account after switching. Each is a day of work plus state filing fees. The math closes inside a year.

## What about UPS Store mailboxes?

A UPS Store PMB is a step up — it accepts packages and reads as a street address. But it's still a CMRA mailbox rental on public filings, and like a PO Box it has no one authorized to accept legal service, so it doesn't satisfy the Texas registered-office rule on its own. Texas banks and payment processors increasingly flag UPS Store addresses during commercial verification, and showing one on a Houston business contract reads like "this is a UPS Store" — a credibility mismatch.

## Quick answers Houston founders ask

**Can I file my Texas LLC with a PO Box?** Not as the registered office. The registered agent needs a physical Texas street address per BOC §5.201; a PO Box can only ever be a secondary mailing line.

**Will a virtual office address work for Google Business Profile?** Yes, when it's a real staffed building with a suite number — which is what a virtual office gives you and a PO Box can't.
