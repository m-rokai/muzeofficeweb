## What each one actually is

A **PO Box** is a numbered box at a USPS Post Office. The traditional format looks like `PO Box 12345, Houston TX 77019`. USPS also offers Street Addressing and other premium services at some locations, so carrier and package options depend on that Post Office.

A **virtual office** is a commercial street address with mail services and, depending on the provider, package receiving or meeting access. ## The Texas rule that settles most of this

Texas does not let you use a PO Box where it matters most: the registered agent. Section 5.201 of the Texas Business Organizations Code requires every entity's registered office to be a **physical street address in Texas** where the agent can be served with legal process during business hours. The Secretary of State is explicit that a registered office may not be a mailbox service or a telephone answering service. A PO Box fails on both counts — it isn't a physical office, and nobody is there to accept service.

That distinction is important: a registered office must be the business office of the appointed registered agent. You can sometimes list a PO Box as a separate *mailing* address, but neither a PO Box nor a virtual office that is not acting as your registered agent satisfies that role. ## What setup actually involves (and why it's not instant)

Before a CMRA receives mail for you, USPS requires a completed [PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf) and the required identity documents. The current form permits signing in the physical or live virtual presence of the CMRA's authorized employee, or acknowledgment before a notary. Provider review time varies. A PO Box uses USPS's own application process instead. For the full sequence, see [how to set up a virtual office in Houston](/blog/how-to-set-up-a-virtual-office-in-houston).

## When a PO Box is fine in Texas

A PO Box is the right call if you:

- Run a hobby business or side project that won't be filed as an LLC
- Only need a private address for personal mail
- Don't need to receive packages from any commercial carrier
- Don't need package receiving, meeting space, or a commercial mailing address

If that's you, save the $25–$30 a month — the cheaper option genuinely does the job.

## When you need a virtual office in Houston instead

Start with a virtual office whenever you're:

- Receiving packages or needing a commercial mailing address for contracts
- Booking occasional client meetings or workspace time
- Opening an account whose institution accepts the address type
- Selling products that ship via UPS, FedEx, or Amazon
- Listing an address on contracts, invoices, or a public-facing website
- Working with platforms that require address verification
- Holding a Texas professional license (TREC, insurance, etc.)

Use the address that meets the specific requirement in front of you. For Texas formation, confirm the registered-agent and business-address fields with the Secretary of State or a Texas attorney; banks and platforms each set their own verification rules.

## What about UPS Store mailboxes?

A private mailbox can add package handling and a street-style format, but it remains a CMRA service. Unless the appointed registered agent actually maintains its business office there, it does not satisfy the Texas registered-office rule. Banks, agencies, and platforms decide how they classify and verify the address.

## Quick answers Houston founders ask

**Can I file my Texas LLC with a PO Box?** Not as the registered office. The registered agent needs a physical Texas street address per BOC §5.201; a PO Box can only ever be a secondary mailing line.

**Will a virtual office address work for Google Business Profile?** Not by itself. Google requires the business to operate at the location, receive customers during stated hours, be staffed by its own team, and maintain permanent signage.
