### Why a Las Vegas address in the first place?

Nevada is usually a deliberate choice. The state has **no personal income tax, no corporate income tax, and no franchise tax** on most small businesses, which is why a steady stream of out-of-state founders, remote LLCs, and e-commerce operators register here. A virtual office can supply a commercial mailing address, while a registered agent remains a separate legal requirement.

## Step 2 — Sign up and complete USPS Form 1583

USPS requires Form 1583 before a commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA) can accept mail on your behalf. We send the current form when you sign up. You'll need:

- An unexpired government-issued photo ID from the form's accepted list
- A second form of identification that verifies your home address and appears on the form's accepted list
- Identity verification in the CMRA employee's physical or live virtual presence, or acknowledgment before a notary

Mail service begins after the provider accepts your completed form and identification. Do not use the address on applications until activation is confirmed.

## Step 3 — Update your LLC and state registrations

After activation, the address may be used on applications and accounts that accept a commercial mail-receiving address, including potentially:

- Nevada Secretary of State LLC filings (Articles of Organization, Annual List)
- IRS EIN application (Form SS-4)
- State business license
- Sales tax permit (if applicable)

Your registered agent is a separate role — Muze Office is your business address, not your registered agent. If you don't have a registered agent yet, services like Northwest or Harbor Compliance handle that piece.

Budget for the recurring Nevada costs, which catch first-time filers off guard. Every Nevada LLC owes a **$150 Annual List fee** plus a **$200 State Business License renewal** each year — $350 total — due by the last day of your LLC's anniversary month, with a $100 penalty if the business-license portion is late. That's a state obligation separate from your virtual office plan.

## Step 4 — Understand Google Business Profile rules

A virtual-office-only address does not make a business eligible for a Google Business Profile. Google requires the business itself to operate at the location, receive customers during stated hours, be staffed by its own team, and maintain permanent signage. If you meet those requirements through a genuine workspace arrangement, review Google's current guidelines before applying; Muze Office cannot guarantee verification or listing eligibility.

## Step 5 — Update your bank and payment processors

Banks and payment platforms set their own physical-address, mailing-address, and identity-verification rules. Some accept a CMRA address only as a mailing address and require another physical address. Check the current requirements with each institution before applying; Form 1583 does not guarantee approval.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming every filing accepts the same address type.** Nevada requires a physical street address for a registered agent, while business and mailing fields can have different rules. Check each field before filing.
- **Skipping Form 1583.** Federal law — non-negotiable. Mail won't be accepted until it's on file.
- **Listing the virtual office as your registered agent.** It isn't. Get a separate registered agent service.
- **Forgetting to update your existing accounts.** Mail sent to your old address won't be forwarded automatically by USPS forever.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to live in Nevada to use a Las Vegas virtual office?**
No. You can complete Form 1583 through the provider's live virtual verification process or, where available, acknowledgment before an online notary, without traveling to Nevada. If you do visit, the building is open to you.

**Is this a legal address for an LLC, or just a mailbox?**
It's a commercial street address at a real, staffed building. It is not your registered agent, and the requirements for LLC filings vary by form and purpose. Confirm which address is permitted with the Nevada Secretary of State or a Nevada attorney before filing.

**How is a virtual office different from a coworking membership?**
A virtual office is an address and mail service — you're not paying for a desk. A coworking or [private office](/las-vegas-private-office) membership pays for space to actually work. Many members start with a virtual office, then add a Day Pass or meeting-room hours only when they need them. You can run both at once.

**What happens to mail and packages when they arrive?**

**Can I open a U.S. business bank account with this address?**
