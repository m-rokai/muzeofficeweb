### Why a Las Vegas address in the first place?

Nevada is usually a deliberate choice. The state has **no personal income tax, no corporate income tax, and no franchise tax** on most small businesses, which is why a steady stream of out-of-state founders, remote LLCs, and e-commerce operators register here. A virtual office gives you the qualifying physical street address that decision requires, without the cost of a leased suite you'd never use.

It also matters that the address is a *real, staffed* commercial building rather than a mailbox-store cubby. Our address — **6860 Bermuda Rd, Suite 200**, in the Enterprise/Paradise area near the airport — is an actual office with on-site staff who sign for couriers, an on-site cafe, and free parking. That distinction helps later: when you verify a Google Business Profile or open a bank account, a genuine staffed building clears review more cleanly than an address flagged as a pure mail drop.

## Step 2 — Sign up and complete USPS Form 1583

Federal law requires a notarized USPS Form 1583 before any commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA) can accept mail on your behalf. We send the form when you sign up. You'll need:

- A photo ID (driver's license or passport)
- A second ID (utility bill, lease, or second photo ID)
- A notary — online services like NotaryCam or Notarize work; so does a UPS Store

Upload the notarized form back to us. From that point your address is live.

## Step 3 — Update your LLC and state registrations

Use **6860 Bermuda Rd, Suite 200, Las Vegas NV 89119** as your business address on:

- Nevada Secretary of State LLC filings (Articles of Organization, Annual List)
- IRS EIN application (Form SS-4)
- State business license
- Sales tax permit (if applicable)

Your registered agent is a separate role — Muze Office is your business address, not your registered agent. If you don't have a registered agent yet, services like Northwest or Harbor Compliance handle that piece.

Budget for the recurring Nevada costs, which catch first-time filers off guard. Every Nevada LLC owes a **$150 Annual List fee** plus a **$200 State Business License renewal** each year — $350 total — due by the last day of your LLC's anniversary month, with a $100 penalty if the business-license portion is late. That's a state obligation separate from your virtual office plan.

## Step 4 — Verify your Google Business Profile

A virtual office in Las Vegas is one of the few address types Google Business Profile will accept that isn't a leased commercial space. Submit the address, request verification (postcard or video), and we'll receive the verification mail at the suite. We don't open it — we notify you and you pick it up or have it forwarded.

GBP verification takes 5–14 days end to end.

## Step 5 — Update your bank and payment processors

Banks vary. Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo generally accept commercial mail receiving agency addresses on business checking accounts; some online-first banks (Mercury, Brex) prefer them; a few traditional lenders won't. Check before you switch. Stripe, Square, and PayPal accept the address with documentation showing you've completed Form 1583.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Filing the LLC with a P.O. Box first.** The Nevada Secretary of State will reject this. Use the virtual office address from the start.
- **Skipping Form 1583.** Federal law — non-negotiable. Mail won't be accepted until it's on file.
- **Listing the virtual office as your registered agent.** It isn't. Get a separate registered agent service.
- **Forgetting to update your existing accounts.** Mail sent to your old address won't be forwarded automatically by USPS forever.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to live in Nevada to use a Las Vegas virtual office?**
No — the most common setup is the opposite: an out-of-state or international owner who wants a Nevada-registered entity. You complete Form 1583 remotely with an online notary and never have to set foot in the state. If you do visit, the building is open to you.

**Is this a legal address for an LLC, or just a mailbox?**
It's a legal commercial street address at a real, staffed building — what the Nevada Secretary of State, the IRS, and Google Business Profile expect. It is *not* a P.O. Box (those get rejected on LLC filings) and *not* your registered agent (a separate role you still need to assign).

**How is a virtual office different from a coworking membership?**
A virtual office is an address and mail service — you're not paying for a desk. A coworking or [private office](/las-vegas-private-office) membership pays for space to actually work. Many members start with a virtual office, then add a Day Pass or meeting-room hours only when they need them. You can run both at once.

**What happens to mail and packages when they arrive?**
On Mail Holding we receive and hold your items, then notify you for pickup. Higher tiers (Sandstone, Opal, Diamond) add courier/parcel acceptance and forwarding. Staff sign for UPS, FedEx, and Amazon deliveries during business hours — useful for e-commerce sellers who can't risk a doorstep drop.

**Can I open a U.S. business bank account with this address?**
Generally yes. Most major and online-first banks accept a commercial mail receiving agency address once Form 1583 is on file (see Step 5). Always confirm with your specific bank first.
