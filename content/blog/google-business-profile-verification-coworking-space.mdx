Google My Business is now called **Google Business Profile**, but the question we hear has not changed: can a business verify its Google listing at a coworking space? If you are looking for Google My Business verification instructions, this is the current version of that process.

The short answer is **sometimes—but renting an address is not enough**.

Google distinguishes between a business that genuinely operates from a coworking location and one that only uses the address for mail. A qualifying business may need to prove its physical presence, permanent signage, customer access, staffing, and control of the workspace. Google chooses which verification methods are available, and the process can go beyond receiving a postcard or entering a code.

We have seen that happen firsthand at Muze Office.

## What One Muze Office Member Had to Show Google

One of our members experienced problems while verifying a Google Business Profile for a business operating from our coworking space. The case eventually moved to a live video call with a Google representative.

During the call, the member was asked to show:

- Signage for their business that was **"permanently affixed"** in the workspace
- An identifiable directory showing the business at the location
- The physical space demonstrating that the company actually operated there

The purpose was not simply to prove that the building existed or that mail could arrive there. The representative wanted evidence that this particular business had a real, identifiable presence inside the coworking location.

That was one member's experience, not a universal script. Google's published rules require permanent fixed signage for businesses displaying an address, but they do not state that every applicant must appear on a building directory. Google may request different proof depending on the business, location, public information, and verification method.

## Eligibility Comes Before Verification

Verification proves that you manage an eligible business. It does not turn an ineligible address into an eligible one.

Under Google's current [guidelines for representing a business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en), a business cannot create a profile at a virtual office when it rents the mailing address but does not operate there. Google says an office in a coworking space can be listed only when it:

- Maintains clear signage
- Receives customers at the location during business hours
- Is staffed during business hours by the business's own staff

Google also says businesses that display their address should maintain permanent fixed signage showing the business name at that address.

Those are operational requirements, not just items to stage for a verification video. A temporary paper sign, an occasional meeting-room booking, mail reception by coworking staff, or a virtual-office membership by itself does not establish that the business operates at the location.

### Coworking office versus virtual office

A coworking provider can offer several different products at the same address, but Google evaluates how **your business** uses the location.

| Arrangement | What it generally means for Google Business Profile |
|---|---|
| Mail or virtual-office service only | Not eligible at that address if the business does not operate there |
| Occasional room or day-pass use | Does not by itself demonstrate an ongoing business location |
| Coworking or office membership where the business genuinely operates | May be eligible if signage, customer access, staffing, and other guidelines are satisfied |
| Service-area business that visits customers | May qualify with its address hidden instead of presenting the coworking space as a storefront |
| Online-only business | Not eligible for a Business Profile |

The membership name on an invoice is not the deciding factor. The real-world operation is.

## What Google May Ask You to Prove

Google automatically determines the verification options available to each profile. Its current [verification overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) lists methods that may include phone or text, email, live video, video recording, or postcard. Some businesses may be asked to complete more than one method.

If you are asked for video verification at a coworking location, prepare to establish three things.

### 1. The business is at the stated location

Google's video guidance asks businesses to show location details such as street signs, building numbers, nearby businesses, or other recognizable surroundings. At a multi-tenant building, it helps to make the path understandable from the exterior to your workspace.

An identifiable building or suite directory can reinforce that connection. Based on our member's experience, it can also become a specific request during a live call. Treat it as useful supporting evidence, not as a substitute for Google's other eligibility requirements.

### 2. The business exists there

For a storefront or hybrid business, Google's [video-recording requirements](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705?hl=en) call for the business name to be clearly shown on a permanent fixture such as a signboard, wall, or window. The name should match the Business Profile.

In a coworking environment, that means a branded sign taped to a wall for the duration of the recording is a poor foundation. Discuss approved, permanent signage with the workspace operator before starting verification.

Depending on the business, Google may also expect to see the products, equipment, tools, or branded materials used to serve customers.

### 3. You manage the business and workspace

Google may ask for evidence that only an owner or authorized employee would normally have. Examples in Google's guidance include unlocking the facility, accessing an employee-only area, operating a point-of-sale system, or showing non-sensitive business documents.

At a coworking office, a key, access credential, lockable office, business equipment, or other legitimate proof of control may help. Avoid displaying bank account numbers, tax IDs, government identification numbers, other people's faces, or confidential customer information.

## Permanent Signage Matters More Than Many Businesses Expect

The phrase that stood out during our member's call was **"permanently affixed."**

Google's published policy uses similar language: businesses showing their address should maintain permanent fixed signage of the business name. Its video instructions say the name should appear on a permanent fixture.

Before applying, check that:

- The sign displays the same real-world business name used on the profile
- The sign is installed in a place associated with the business's workspace
- The sign is not a temporary printout created only for verification
- The signage complies with the coworking operator's rules and lease or membership terms
- The sign remains in place after verification

Verification is not the finish line. A business may be asked to re-verify after changing important profile information, and profiles that no longer comply can be suspended.

## A Practical Verification Checklist for Coworking Tenants

Use this checklist before selecting **Get verified**:

### Confirm the business is actually eligible

- Customers can visit the location during the hours shown on the profile
- Your own business staff—not only the coworking provider's receptionist—are present during those hours
- The business genuinely operates from the location
- The address and suite information are precise
- The profile represents one real business, not an extra listing created only to rank in another city

If those statements are not true, stop and reconsider the profile setup.

### Make the physical presence identifiable

- Install approved permanent signage showing the exact business name
- Ask whether the property has a tenant directory and whether your business can be listed
- Confirm that the exterior building number and suite are visible or easy to demonstrate
- Make sure visitors can reasonably locate the business

A directory entry is useful, but it does not cure missing signage, staffing, or customer access.

### Make your business information consistent

Check the profile against your:

- Website
- Signage
- Business phone number
- Hours
- Suite or office information
- Licenses, invoices, or other non-sensitive business records you may use as supporting evidence

Small inconsistencies can make a shared address harder to understand.

### Plan the video route

For a recorded video, Google currently requires one continuous, unedited recording of at least 30 seconds, captured and uploaded from a mobile device through the Business Profile.

Plan a route that can show, without cuts:

1. The street, building number, or nearby location markers
2. The building entrance and route to the workspace
3. A directory entry, if available
4. Permanent business signage
5. The actual working area, equipment, or products
6. Proof that you control or manage the business space

Do not pre-record the video in your camera app. Google's process requires recording and uploading through the profile.

### Prepare for a live call

Live video verification is not available to every business, but if Google offers it:

- Be physically present at the business
- Use a phone with a stable connection and working camera
- Schedule the call when your location is open
- Make sure an authorized owner or employee can access the relevant areas
- Be ready to walk outside and back through the location
- Have non-sensitive supporting documents available in case they are requested

The representative may direct the call differently from the route you planned. Follow the instructions shown in your profile or provided by Google support.

## Common Reasons a Coworking-Based Profile Runs Into Trouble

### The business only receives mail there

A staffed coworking building is still not automatically the operating location of every company receiving mail inside it. A virtual-office-only arrangement is not eligible for a profile at that address.

### The sign is temporary

A removable printout or sign installed only for the video does not demonstrate the permanent real-world presence described in Google's guidelines.

### The coworking provider's staff are treated as the business's staff

Google specifically refers to staffing by **your business staff**. Mail reception, guest greeting, or administrative support supplied by the coworking operator does not mean your company is staffed there during its listed hours.

### Customers are not actually received there

If your company never meets or serves customers at the location, showing the address as a customer-facing storefront may be inaccurate. A qualifying service-area business should generally hide its address and define the area it serves.

### The profile name does not match the real-world business

Adding a city, service, tagline, or other keywords to the profile name can create a mismatch with signage and business documents. Use the name customers encounter in the real world.

### The video proves the building, but not the business

Showing the coworking brand, lobby, and street address proves that the workspace exists. Google may still need evidence that your company—not only the coworking provider—operates inside it.

## What a Coworking Provider Can and Cannot Do

A coworking provider may be able to help you understand available workspace options, approved signage locations, directory procedures, visitor access, and building logistics. It can also make it easier to show a real commercial setting when your business legitimately operates there.

The provider cannot:

- Make a mail-only business eligible
- Supply your company's required staffing
- Promise a particular verification method
- Approve a profile on Google's behalf
- Guarantee verification or prevent a future suspension

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I verify Google Business Profile with a coworking address?

Potentially, if the business genuinely operates there and meets Google's coworking requirements for clear signage, customer access, and staffing by its own team during business hours. Renting the address alone is not enough.

### Does a building directory prove my business is eligible?

No. A directory can help identify your business at a shared location, and one Muze Office member was asked to show it during a live verification call. It does not replace permanent signage, actual operations, customer access, or staffing.

### Does the coworking receptionist count as my staff?

Google's guidelines say a coworking office must be staffed during business hours by **your business staff**. Do not assume the workspace operator's team satisfies that requirement.

### Can I request a live video call?

Google automatically determines the verification methods available for a profile. Live video is one possible option, but it may not appear for every business. Use the options shown on your profile and contact Google support if you remain unable to verify.

### What happens if my verification video is rejected?

Google may show a **Review issues** notice explaining what was missing. Review the feedback, correct the underlying issue, and record a new video that includes both the original evidence and the missing information. Google's current guidance says reviews can take up to five business days after submission.

### Is a Google Business Profile still called Google My Business?

Google renamed Google My Business to Google Business Profile. People still use both terms, but Google's current help documentation and product interface use Google Business Profile.

## The Bottom Line

A coworking address is not a shortcut into Google Maps. It can support a legitimate Business Profile when the business truly works from the location and satisfies Google's rules.

Our member's live call made the standard tangible: Google wanted to see permanently affixed signage, an identifiable directory, and a real operating presence in the space. The strongest preparation is not a clever verification tactic. It is making sure the profile accurately reflects how the business operates every day.
