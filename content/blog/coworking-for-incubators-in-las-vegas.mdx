Las Vegas has quietly become a real place to build a company, not just visit one. Nevada's tax structure is a big reason why: the state has no personal income tax and no corporate income tax, and the Nevada Commerce Tax only applies to businesses with more than $4 million in Nevada gross revenue in a taxable year ([Nevada Department of Taxation](https://tax.nv.gov/about-nevada-department-of-taxation/income-tax-in-nevada/)). For an early-stage startup or an incubator cohort, that means more of your runway stays in the business. The harder part is the physical and human infrastructure: a place to put a small team, rooms to meet mentors and investors, and a community that keeps founders from working in isolation. That is exactly the gap coworking fills — and it is what this guide covers, including how Muze Office at **6860 Bermuda Rd, Suite 200** fits into the Las Vegas startup picture.

## Why Las Vegas works for incubators and early-stage teams

The city has a genuine, organized startup ecosystem. [StartUpNV](https://startupnv.org/), a nonprofit statewide accelerator and incubator founded in 2017, runs co-working and programming in Las Vegas and Reno, operates affiliated venture funds (FundNV, AngelNV, and the 1864 Seed Fund), and in 2026 took over management of the International Innovation Center in downtown Las Vegas ([Nevada Business Magazine](https://nevadabusiness.com/2026/05/startupnv-takes-over-management-of-international-innovation-center-in-downtown-las-vegas/)). That matters for incubators because the surrounding capital and mentor network already exists — your job is to plug your founders into it, not to build it from scratch.

A flexible workspace is the practical layer underneath that ecosystem. Incubator cohorts change size every few months: a program might run six teams of two, then graduate half and intake a new batch. A traditional office lease, with a multi-year commitment and a fixed footprint, is the wrong shape for that. Coworking is month-to-month and resizable, so the space matches the cohort instead of fighting it.

### What an incubator actually needs from a workspace

- **Room to flex headcount.** Day passes for visiting mentors, hot desks for solo founders, and a couple of private offices for teams that need to lock a door.
- **Meeting rooms on demand.** Pitch practice, advisor sessions, and first investor calls need a real room with a door and good WiFi — not a coffee-shop table.
- **A reason for founders to show up.** An on-site cafe, phone booths, and a shared floor create the unplanned conversations that early-stage teams learn from.
- **No long-term lease.** When a cohort ends or pivots, the space commitment should end or change with it.

## Common questions from founders and program leads

**Can a small team start on day passes and scale up later?**
Yes. Many early teams begin with [day passes](/blog/day-pass-coworking-las-vegas) or hot desks, add a private office as headcount grows, and keep meeting rooms on an hourly basis. Because everything is month-to-month, you are never locked into more space than the cohort currently needs.

**Where do mentors and investors actually meet us?**
In a booked meeting room with a door, reliable WiFi, and a phone booth nearby for follow-up calls. For a larger session, the Classroom accommodates up to 50 people at $99/hour, while the Event Space is $199/hour for a demo day, cohort kickoff, or investor mixer.

**Do we need a Nevada address before we have customers?**
A virtual office gives you a legitimate, staffed Las Vegas address from $39/month, which is useful for entity registration, business banking, and keeping a home address off public filings. It is far cheaper than renting desks before the team is full.

### A note on focus and founder well-being

Burnout is a real failure mode for early-stage teams, and isolation makes it worse. Research from the American Psychological Association found that [workers without control or flexibility over their schedules reported a negative impact on their mental health](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/work-in-america/2023-workplace-health-well-being#:~:text=Work%2Dlife%20harmony%20is,have%20that%20flexibility%20\(23%25\).). A flexible, staffed space with 24/7 access lets founders work on their own rhythm while still having people around — a better default than a spare bedroom or a noisy cafe.
