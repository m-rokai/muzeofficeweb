The NAB Show brings media, entertainment, and broadcast professionals to the Las Vegas Convention Center each spring. The [2027 show](https://amplify.nabshow.com/las-vegas/) runs **April 3–7, with exhibits open April 4–7**, at the Las Vegas Convention Center. Attendees can check the organizer's site for the current program and registration details.

The show brings together content creators, broadcast engineers, filmmakers, podcasters, streaming teams, and media buyers. If you have deadlines or private meetings between sessions, it helps to plan a workspace away from the show floor.

Muze Office is about **10 minutes from the LVCC** at 6860 Bermuda Rd, Suite 200, off-Strip in the Enterprise/Paradise area — with fast dedicated WiFi, private phone booths, free on-site parking, and meeting rooms equipped for video conferencing.

## Why NAB Show attendees need off-site workspace

Many NAB attendees still have production deadlines while they are at the show. Editors may need to upload large files; producers may need a room for interviews; sales teams may need privacy for licensing discussions. Those tasks are easier to schedule if you know where you can work between sessions.

Here's who tends to need real space during NAB week:

- **Video editors and post-production staff** need a real desk with strong upload speeds to cut footage, assemble sizzle reels, or push dailies to a team back home. A hotel-room desk and shared lobby WiFi won't move a 4K timeline.
- **Podcast and audio producers** need a quiet room with a door — ideally away from the hum of a convention hall — to record interviews with guests they just met at the show, while the conversation is still fresh.
- **Broadcast engineers and systems integrators** reviewing equipment specs and integration plans need a focused desk, a printer, and a stable connection, not a crowded coffee bar.
- **Media buyers, sellers, and distribution teams** negotiating programming, content licensing, and platform deals need private meeting rooms where pricing and terms stay confidential — not a booth where competitors can overhear.
- **Production teams** running the show as a group need somewhere to hold a daily standup and review footage on a screen bigger than a laptop.

The common thread is that the most valuable NAB conversations and the most demanding NAB tasks both need privacy and bandwidth — and both are scarce on the show floor and in a Strip hotel during the busiest media week of the year.

## Getting to Muze Office from the LVCC

The Las Vegas Convention Center sits just off the Strip; Muze Office is off-Strip in the Paradise area, which means you skip the worst of the convention crush.

- **Drive:** About 10 minutes via I-215, with the Bermuda Rd exit just a couple of minutes from our door. Parking at Muze is always free, while convention-center and Strip parking during a major show typically runs $20-40 a day on top of your hotel.
- **Rideshare:** Roughly $15-25 from the LVCC, depending on surge pricing during peak show hours.
- **Airport:** Muze is only about 10 minutes from Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) via I-215 — handy if you're flying in for a single day of meetings or screenings.

## Day pass or meeting room — what NAB attendees should book

For most NAB attendees, the right call depends on what you're producing:

- **If you're a solo editor, producer, or engineer** who mainly needs a quiet desk and reliable upload bandwidth between sessions, the **$25 day pass is the better value.** You get the desk, the WiFi, the coffee, free parking, and phone booths for any private calls — without paying for a room you'll only use part of the day.
- **If you're recording interviews, running demos, or negotiating deals,** book a **meeting room from $39/hr.** The closed door is the point: it protects audio quality for recordings and confidentiality for licensing and distribution conversations.
- **If your team needs a base for the whole show,** ask about private-office availability and membership terms. A lockable room may help when several people need to regroup between sessions.

A few logistics tips: reserve meeting rooms before you arrive (mid-morning and late-afternoon slots between sessions fill first), and use the cafe and phone booths for working lunches and confidential calls instead of sharing a table with tens of thousands of other attendees.
