The NAB Show (National Association of Broadcasters) is the premier global event for media, entertainment, and broadcast technology. Every April, tens of thousands of content creators, broadcast engineers, filmmakers, podcasters, streaming specialists, and media executives converge on the Las Vegas Convention Center to see new production technology, attend sessions, and close deals. The 2026 edition runs **April 18–22, with exhibits open April 19–22**, at the Las Vegas Convention Center on Paradise Road, according to [NAB Show](https://www.nabshow.com/las-vegas/).

This is a genuinely large show. The 2025 edition drew about **55,000 registered attendees from 160 countries**, with nearly 1,100 exhibitors — and notably, roughly half of attendees were first-timers, per [TV Tech](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/2025-nab-show-attracts-55-000-registered-attendees). That scale matters for one practical reason: every hotel lobby, business center, and convention-floor work nook near the LVCC gets pushed to capacity for the week. If you actually need to get work done between sessions, you need somewhere off the convention radar.

## Why NAB Show attendees need off-site workspace

NAB attendees have a workspace need that most conventions don't: they work with large files and need quiet, controlled environments. A media professional doesn't stop producing because they're at a trade show — deadlines back home don't pause for the exhibit hall. The 2026 program leans heavily into AI workflows, streaming, sports production, the creator economy, and cloud-based pipelines, which means a large share of the floor is people whose day job is moving heavy media through tight turnarounds.

Here's who tends to need real space during NAB week:

- **Video editors and post-production staff** need a real desk with strong upload speeds to cut footage, assemble sizzle reels, or push dailies to a team back home. A hotel-room desk and shared lobby WiFi won't move a 4K timeline.
- **Podcast and audio producers** need a quiet room with a door — ideally away from the hum of a convention hall — to record interviews with guests they just met at the show, while the conversation is still fresh.
- **Broadcast engineers and systems integrators** reviewing equipment specs and integration plans need a focused desk, a printer, and a stable connection, not a crowded coffee bar.
- **Media buyers, sellers, and distribution teams** negotiating programming, content licensing, and platform deals need private meeting rooms where pricing and terms stay confidential — not a booth where competitors can overhear.
- **Production teams** running the show as a group need somewhere to hold a daily standup and review footage on a screen bigger than a laptop.

The common thread is that the most valuable NAB conversations and the most demanding NAB tasks both need privacy and bandwidth — and both are scarce on the show floor and in a Strip hotel during the busiest media week of the year.
