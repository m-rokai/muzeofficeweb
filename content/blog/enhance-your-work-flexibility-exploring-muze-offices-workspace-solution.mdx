"Flexible workspace" is one of those phrases that gets used so broadly it stops meaning anything. For a freelancer it might mean a hot desk for an afternoon; for a remote employee it might mean a quiet room with real WiFi away from the kitchen table; for a growing company it might mean a private office without signing a five-year lease. The honest answer is that "flexible" should mean you only pay for what you actually use, and you can change your mind next month without penalty. That's the standard we hold ourselves to at Muze Office in Las Vegas, and below we walk through what that looks like in practice, what it costs, and how to figure out which option fits you.

## What "flexible" actually means at Muze Office

The core of our model is month-to-month with no long-term lease. Whether you book a single day or take a private office, you are not locked into a contract you'll regret in six months. That single decision shapes everything else: pricing is transparent and per-use, you can scale up or down as your work changes, and you can test the space before committing.

## A few questions people actually ask

**Do I have to commit to anything?**
No. Everything is month-to-month, and a day pass requires no membership at all. The point of a flexible workspace is that you can change your arrangement as your work changes.

**Can I use Muze as my business address without renting a desk?**
Yes — that's what a [virtual office](/las-vegas-virtual-office) is for. Plans start at $39/month for mail handling, with higher tiers (Sandstone $69, Opal $149, Diamond $249) adding services like mail forwarding and meeting-room time. Nevada has no state personal income tax, no corporate income tax, and no franchise tax on most small businesses, which is a real part of why out-of-state founders register here in the first place.

**What if I just need a room for one important meeting?**
Book a [meeting room](/las-vegas-meeting-rooms) by the hour, starting at $39, without becoming a member. It's a common reason people first visit.

**Is this a good fit for a small team?**
Often, yes. A private office gives you a door and privacy on the same month-to-month terms; a [coworking](/las-vegas-coworking) membership suits teams that are comfortable in a shared room. If you're weighing the two, our [private office vs coworking space](/blog/private-office-vs-coworking-space) comparison lays out the trade-offs.

**I'm not in Las Vegas — does Muze cover other cities?**
A Houston location is planned for the Galleria area in 2026, but it is not open and no addresses can be reserved yet. [Join Houston early access](/locations/houston#waitlist) for confirmed updates.

## How to choose without overthinking it

A simple way to decide: if you need the space fewer than a couple of days a month, buy [day passes](/las-vegas-day-pass) as you go. If you're in several days a week, a coworking membership costs less per day. If privacy or a permanent setup matters, look at a private office. And if you only need the address and mail — not the desk — a [virtual office](/las-vegas-virtual-office) is the cheapest path. Because everything is month-to-month, picking wrong is cheap to fix.
